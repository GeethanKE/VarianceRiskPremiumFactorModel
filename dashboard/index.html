Factor Research · Variance Risk Premium

VRP = IV² − RV²

Cross-sectional factor model using the variance risk premium as a return predictor. Replicates and extends Carr & Wu (2009) with post-2015 data, documents post-publication alpha decay, and investigates microstructure explanations.

Model specification
VRP definition (Carr-Wu 2009)
VRPi,t = IV²i,t − RV²i,t
IV²i,t  = model-free implied variance (30-day ATM)
RV²i,t  = realized variance from 5-min intraday returns
Cross-sectional Fama-MacBeth regression
ri,t+1 = λ₀t + λ₁t·VRPi,t + λ₂t·βi,t + λ₃t·log(ME)i,t + εi,t+1

Key findings
01
Replication succeeds in-sample. The original Carr-Wu slope coefficient (+0.018, t = 4.7) is reproduced over 1996–2009 with minor differences attributable to option data cleaning choices.
02
Significant post-publication decay. The VRP premium declines ~67% from in-sample to 2016–2022, with t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds.
03
Crowding and limits-to-arbitrage are the primary decay channels. Growth in vol-selling AUM and option margin costs explain the bulk of premium compression.
04
Crisis-period dependency. Excluding 2008–09 and 2020 nearly eliminates statistical significance, suggesting the premium is episodic rather than structural.
05
Net-of-cost alpha is negative post-2016. After realistic round-trip transaction costs (≥5bps), the strategy produces negative risk-adjusted returns in the post-publication period.

Data: OptionMetrics (IV), TAQ 5-min (RV). Universe: S&P 500 constituents with liquid options. All returns CAPM-adjusted.
